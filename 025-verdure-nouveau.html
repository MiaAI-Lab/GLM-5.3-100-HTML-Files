VERDURE

an ode to a garden at dusk — where the light forgives everything

There is an hour — barely forty minutes, if the weather is generous — when the garden stops performing. The roses let their heads fall. The light comes in low and gold across the lawn, and every leaf seems to remember the sun it ate at noon. This is the garden's true face, and it does not perform for anyone. It simply exhales.

Stand very still and you can watch the shift of color: green upon green upon gold, sage into shadow, the first blue arriving at the edges of things like a rumor. The bees have gone home. Something else rustles — a blackbird turning over leaf litter, deciding about tomorrow.

We built this page the way one tends such a garden: patiently, by hand, with more care than efficiency. Let the vines grow. Let the season turn. There is no hurry here, and nothing to buy.

The dusk does not ask the garden to be beautiful.
It only asks it to be still.

Come back when the light changes. The garden will be here, and so will we — tending the vowels, pruning the consonants, letting the sentences run a little wild at the edges, the way they prefer.
